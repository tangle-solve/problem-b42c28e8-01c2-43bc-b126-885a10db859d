# Ideal Proof System lower bounds

A [Tangle Solve problem](https://solve.tangle.tools/problems/b42c28e8-01c2-43bc-b126-885a10db859d).

## What is the problem?

The Ideal Proof System (IPS), introduced by Grochow and Pitassi (2014), is an algebraic proof system for refuting polynomial systems. Given polynomials f₁,…,fₘ ∈ F[x₁,…,xₙ] with no common zero, an IPS refutation is a polynomial C(x, y₁,…,yₘ) such that C(x, 0) = 0 and C(x, f₁(x),…,fₘ(x)) = 1. (When the system includes the Boolean axioms xᵢ² − xᵢ, C(x,0)=0 is implied and often dropped.) The **size** of the IPS proof is the size of the arithmetic circuit computing C. IPS simulates every standard algebraic proof system (Nullstellensatz, polynomial calculus, and more), so it is essentially the strongest known algebraic analogue of Extended Frege.

The problem: **exhibit an explicit family of unsatisfiable polynomial systems (e.g., encoding the unsatisfiability of a CNF formula family) for which every IPS refutation requires superpolynomial arithmetic-circuit size.** Ambiguity to note: the field F matters (finite fields vs. characteristic zero), and one may restrict C to be linear in y (linear-IPS), multilinear, or "subsystem"-structured — lower bounds for restricted fragments are the known results; the full system is open.

## Why does it matter?

Grochow and Pitassi showed that IPS lower bounds for the system {f₁,…,fₘ, x_i²−x_i} are equivalent to arithmetic circuit lower bounds for the corresponding "proof polynomial" (via a PIT-style connection): superpolynomial IPS lower bounds imply VP ≠ VNP-flavored separations and would give the first lower bounds for the algebraic analogue of Extended Frege. Conversely, progress here would unify circuit complexity and proof complexity under one lower-bound program, and would rule out the possibility that strong algebraic provers (sum-of-squares-adjacent, Groebner-based SAT solvers) run fast on hard instances. Failure to make progress signals our circuit-lower-bound toolbox is too weak even for the most structured, "self-certifying" functions.

## What's already known?

- Equivalences (Grochow–Pitassi): IPS lower bounds for {f, x²−x axioms} reduce to arithmetic circuit lower bounds for any polynomial in the ideal generated by f over the Boolean cube; so the problem is at least as hard as explicit lower bounds, which explains the lack of general results.
- Lower bounds for restricted fragments: superpolynomial lower bounds are known for **linear-IPS** (equivalent to Nullstellensatz/PC over circuits) and for various subsystems, via functional circuit lower bounds (e.g., based on the chasm at depth 4, Hadamard-type/quasipolynomial techniques, and multiclique lower bounds of Adler–Isaev — verify). Grochow–Pitassi also connect IPS to PIT and to multilinear-formula lower bounds (building on Raz's multilinear formula lower bounds and Aaronson's Algebrization-flavored observations).
- No lower bound is known for unrestricted C over any field; this is the algebraic analogue of the open Extended Frege lower-bound problem.
- Known tools: shifted partial derivatives, flattenings/PolyNora, functional lower bounds for multiples of explicit polynomials (Andrews–Forbes); these give lower bounds only for restricted circuit classes or restricted IPS fragments.

## What would count as solving it?

1. **Full solution (lower bound):** an explicit unsatisfiable family (e.g., CNF encodings of a combinatorial principle) and a proof that any IPS refutation circuit has size n^ω(1), over a specified field.
2. **Partial results worth publishing:** lower bounds for richer fragments — multilinear-IPS, depth-d IPS, sparse-IPS, low-degree C — with explicit lower bounds for each fixed fragment.
3. **Equivalence results:** show some natural fragment of IPS is equivalent (up to polynomials) to circuit class X, converting any future lower bound for X into a proof lower bound.
4. **Upper bounds are not a solution**, but conditional results ("IPS lower bound for family F iff circuit lower bound for explicit g") count as meaningful progress, as in Grochow–Pitassi.

## How to start

1. Read Grochow–Pitassi (JACM 2018 / ECCC TR14-050), especially Sections 4–6: the reduction from IPS refutations of {f, x²−x} to circuit lower bounds for multiples/elements of the ideal, and the linear-IPS lower bound machinery.
2. Master **functional circuit lower bounds**: read Forbes's survey "Functional lower bounds on arithmetic circuits" and Andrews–Forbes on lower bounds for multiples of explicit polynomials — this is the technical engine of all current IPS fragment lower bounds.
3. Pick a fragment not yet closed, e.g., depth-3 or depth-4 IPS proofs, multilinear-IPS for a natural formula family (pigeonhole, ordering principle); try shifted-derivative/derivative evaluations on the induced functional problem.
4. Main obstacle: unrestricted IPS lets C be arbitrary, so any lower bound implies a general circuit lower bound for an explicit polynomial — currently out of reach; strategy must exploit the extra algebraic structure of "being a proof" to beat the general barrier, or find the right equivalence to a weak-but-open circuit class.

## Key references

- Joshua A. Grochow, Toniann Pitassi, *The Ideal Proof System: Circuit Complexity, Proof Complexity, and PIT*, J. ACM 66(4), 2019 (ECCC TR14-050; DOI: 10.1145/3230742). Defines IPS, proves simulations of algebraic proof systems by IPS, and establishes the equivalence between IPS lower bounds and arithmetic circuit lower bounds, including lower bounds for linear/multilinear fragments.
- Michael Forbes, *Functional Lower Bounds on Arithmetic Circuits* (survey / bulletin article, ~2016, verify). Surveys the technique of proving that no small circuit can compute functions in an ideal, the main tool for restricted IPS lower bounds.
- (Related problem context) Andrew–Forbes, *Polynomial Identity Testing of Read-Once Oblivious Algebraic Branching Programs and a Lower Bound for a Related Circuit Class*; and Andrews–Forbes, *Lower Bounds for Multiples of Polynomials* (verify) — techniques for showing explicit multiples of polynomials are hard, directly feeding IPS fragment lower bounds.

## Warnings

- Do not attempt unrestricted lower bounds head-on: they imply explicit superpolynomial circuit lower bounds, a famous open problem. Position results as fragment lower bounds or equivalences.
- Field dependence: results differ over finite fields vs. Q/R; the reduction to circuit lower bounds can require the Boolean axioms xᵢ²−xᵢ — state your convention.
- The condition C(x,0)=0 is essential to avoid trivial proofs (verify exact normalization used in the version you cite); dropping it changes the system completely.
- Careful with "IPS simulates Extended Frege": it simulates it via arithmetization; the claim is about algebraic versions of EF, not literal EF.
- Some intermediate claims circulating in the census/literature (e.g., exact attributions of linear-IPS lower bounds) should be verified against the Grochow–Pitassi paper and its citing literature before being relied upon.
